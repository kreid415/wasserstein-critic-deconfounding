\section*{Supplementary Note 4: Leiden Implementation Equivalence}

Computing ARI, NMI and the isolated-label $F_1$ score each requires a Leiden resolution
sweep. Because \texttt{scib} performs an independent sweep per metric, and one per
isolated label, we compute each resolution once and let every metric select its own
optimum from the shared clusterings (Methods). The sweep is the single largest cost in
the metric suite: profiled on the pancreas benchmark (16{,}382 cells, six threads) it
accounted for $52.7$\,s of a $137.8$\,s suite, with iLISI ($35.5$\,s) and cLISI
($15.6$\,s) next; these three account for $75\%$ of the suite, with the remaining nine
metrics together costing $33.9$\,s.

We therefore evaluated whether the \texttt{igraph} Leiden implementation could replace
\texttt{scib}'s \texttt{leidenalg} default. The two are equivalent only when the number
of local-moving iterations is matched: \texttt{scanpy}'s \texttt{igraph} flavour defaults
to \texttt{n\_iterations} $=-1$ (iterate to convergence) whereas \texttt{leidenalg} uses
$2$, and an unmatched comparison makes the implementations appear both slower and
non-equivalent. With \texttt{n\_iterations} $=2$ the sweeps agree to floating-point
tolerance on real data:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\hline
Metric & \texttt{igraph} & \texttt{leidenalg} & $|\Delta|$ \\
\hline
ARI                  & $0.001824$ & $0.001709$ & $1.2 \times 10^{-4}$ \\
NMI                  & $0.041626$ & $0.040549$ & $1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ \\
Isolated-label $F_1$ & $0.057143$ & $0.066667$ & $9.5 \times 10^{-3}$ \\
iLISI                & $0.492240$ & $0.492240$ & $0$ \\
cLISI                & $0.209237$ & $0.209237$ & $0$ \\
ASW (batch)          & $0.984511$ & $0.984511$ & $0$ \\
ASW (cell type)      & $0.496143$ & $0.496143$ & $0$ \\
PAGA Spearman        & $0.254663$ & $0.254663$ & $0$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{\textbf{Implementation equivalence on a single fixed latent.} The full metric
suite was evaluated twice on the \emph{same} trained embedding (pancreas, NB backbone
without decoder conditioning, $150$ epochs), varying only the Leiden implementation, so
the comparison isolates the flavour from run-to-run training variability. The five
clustering-free metrics are bit-identical; the three clustering-dependent metrics differ
at the fourth decimal or below. Suite wall time $67.5$\,s versus $104.8$\,s
($1.55\times$); measured on the resolution sweep alone the speedup is $12$--$15\times$,
but the sweep is $38\%$ of the suite so the end-to-end gain is smaller.}
\label{tab:leiden_equiv}
\end{table}

Agreement is not exact for the clustering-dependent metrics, and we state the residual
explicitly rather than describing the substitution as numerically identical. The largest
discrepancy in Table~\ref{tab:leiden_equiv} is the isolated-label $F_1$
($9.5 \times 10^{-3}$), which is the most sensitive of the three because it selects a
resolution independently for each isolated label and those labels are by construction the
smallest cell populations. The same ordering appears on synthetic latents: the two
implementations agree exactly at strong and moderate cluster separation
($|\Delta$ARI$| = 0$ in both) and differ by $|\Delta$ARI$| = 9 \times 10^{-3}$ only in a
degenerate regime constructed so that no clustering recovers the labels.

Three considerations bound the consequence. First, the largest residual is an order of
magnitude below the spread in ARI across backbones within a dataset ($0.088$), the scale
against which any reported difference is judged. Second, it is confined to ARI, NMI and
the isolated-label $F_1$; silhouette, LISI, linear-probe and PAGA metrics involve no
clustering and were bit-identical. Third, the primary comparison reported in this work is
measured on the linear batch probe, which is clustering-free. For context on the scale of
run-to-run variability, two independent training runs of the identical configuration
differ by $\approx 6 \times 10^{-3}$ in iLISI, so the flavour residual is comparable to or
smaller than the seed noise already present in any reported value. Setting the flavour
parameter to \texttt{None} restores strict \texttt{leidenalg} parity for readers who
prefer it.
