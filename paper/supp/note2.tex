\medskip\noindent\textbf{Supplementary Note 2. Training settings.}\par\smallskip
\begingroup\small\setlength{\parskip}{3pt}
\noindent\textit{One model; differences by design.} Plain scVI, the six penalties and the scvi-tools adversary are the same scVI model of scvi-tools 1.4.2 with the same settings within a task (Supplementary Table~S2, Part A): 10 latent dimensions, one hidden layer of 128 units in the encoder and in the decoder, the zero-inflated negative binomial likelihood, 90\% of the cells for training and the rest for validation, minibatches of 128 cells, and scvi-tools' own optimiser for the encoder and decoder (Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, epsilon 0.01, weight decay $10^{-6}$). Plain scVI is this model at strength $\lambda=0$: the same training plan runs scvi-tools' own training step with no penalty. The values in Supplementary Table~S2 are those recorded in the fits' own latent files, and every fit of a method has the same value. The settings that differ between methods by design are the adversary update steps and the adversary optimiser, and each is the method's standard setting (Supplementary Table~S2, Part B). Adversary update steps per encoder step: the Jensen--Shannon discriminator takes one, as discriminators are trained, and the reference, pooled and barycenter critics take five, with a gradient penalty of weight 10, as Wasserstein critics are trained (Gulrajani et al.; main-text reference). Adversary optimiser: the discriminator uses the encoder's optimiser settings (Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, epsilon 0.01, weight decay $10^{-6}$, as scvi-tools trains its adversarial classifier), and the critics use Adam with learning rate $10^{-4}$ and betas 0 and 0.9, the settings of the same reference. MMD and the Sinkhorn divergence have no adversary network, so they take no adversary step. The adversary steps of an encoder step reuse that step's minibatch: its posterior means are computed once and detached, every adversary step is taken on them, and the encoder step that follows uses the same minibatch (Supplementary Note~1).

\noindent\textit{Adversary input and the two input pilots.} Every penalty reads the encoder's posterior mean, without per-dimension standardisation (Supplementary Note~1). Two pilots tested the alternatives, with switching criteria fixed before they were run: a posterior sample instead of the mean, for the three adversarial methods (the discriminator and the reference and pooled critics), and standardising each latent dimension by the minibatch mean and standard deviation of the posterior means, for five methods (those three, MMD and the Sinkhorn divergence). Both ran on the immune and small chromatin-accessibility tasks with both decoders and seeds 100--102, at three strengths per task--decoder--method combination around its matched batch removal, against the sweep fits at the same settings as the default. A combination is evaluable when both its default and its alternative curve reach the matched batch removal $b^*$, and $D$ is its biology at $b^*$ with the alternative minus that with the default. The rule switched only if four criteria held together: at least two-thirds of the combinations evaluable, with an evaluable combination in every task--decoder pair; mean $D$ above 0.01; mean $D$ positive within every task--decoder pair; and no more failed fits with the alternative. The posterior sample lowered biology at $b^*$ by mean $-0.004$ (9 of 12 combinations evaluable) and standardisation by $-0.003$ (14 of 20 evaluable), so neither met the criteria and both defaults were kept. These values are from the decision record recomputed after the Sinkhorn fits were redone (commit 2168d75); the first record (commit cea50ca, the version at commit 10a1ca5) gave $-0.001$ (12 of 12) and $+0.001$ (17 of 20) and the same decision.

\noindent\textit{Epochs, KL warm-up and failed fits.} Every fit trains for the number of epochs given by scvi-tools' rule, $\min(400,\mathrm{round}(20000\times400/n))$ epochs for a task of $n$ cells, with no early stopping, so epochs, minibatch and latent size are equal for every method within a task: 400 epochs on the four tasks with fewer than 20,000 cells and 82 to 246 on the other four (Supplementary Table~S2, Part C). The weight of the latent KL term rises linearly over 400 epochs (scvi-tools' default warm-up), with epochs counted from zero, so in the last of $E$ epochs it is $(E-1)/400$: 0.9975 on the four tasks with 400 epochs, and 0.2025 to 0.6125 on the four shorter tasks, on which the warm-up is not complete. The penalty acts at its full strength from the first step and is not scaled by the warm-up. Every fit is scored on the posterior mean of every cell (Supplementary Note~3). A fit fails if a loss term that a training step computes or back-propagates becomes non-finite, or if its posterior-mean latent holds a non-finite value; failed fits are counted in Supplementary Table~S5.
