## Conflicts with PAPER_PLAN, the July manuscript or earlier reviews

- Under label (composition) shift, alignment with any proper divergence forces errors, and tighter alignment forces more (Zhao 2019 Thm 4.3; Wu 2019) (docs/PAPER_PLAN.md section 2 (Framing), "Theory" bullet): Zhao 2019 Thm 4.3 (PMLR p7) is stated for binary hypotheses with the JS distance on the representation; "tighter forces more" holds in JS-distance terms only. For any divergence the error is forced only at exact alignment (a distance that is zero only for identical distributions), which is Wu 2019 Prop. 3.1 (PMLR p3), also for binary labels. The outline words it this way.
- scIB-E compares GAN, HSIC, orthogonality and MI losses in scVI/scANVI (docs/PAPER_PLAN.md section 2; rigour_review.md A.6): scIB-E level 1 has six losses: GAN, HSIC, orthogonal projection, MIM, reverse backpropagation (gradient reversal) and reverse cross-entropy (Genome Biol 26:398, p4); levels 2-3 add label-aware losses in scANVI. The absence of W1, MMD and OT is confirmed (0 occurrences of Wasserstein, MMD, maximum mean, optimal transport, Sinkhorn in the full text). The outline lists all six.
- In GANs the loss is second-order to tuning and regularisation (Lucic 2018; Kurach 2019) (docs/PAPER_PLAN.md section 2): Lucic 2018 (p1) supports: similar scores with enough tuning and restarts; no algorithm consistently beats the non-saturating GAN. Kurach 2019 supports: the non-saturating loss is stable; spectral normalisation consistently helps; the gradient penalty has no clear trend (p5). Neither paper ranks the loss below regularisation in those words, and both are image-GAN studies. The outline states the findings, not "second-order".
- The JS-vs-W1 premise becomes a tested hypothesis (docs/PAPER_PLAN.md section 3, Introduction item 2): SI-68 (user decision, 2026-10-14) makes the premise check R0 exploratory, with no confirmed or refuted claim. The ledger wins; the outline says the premise is examined, not tested.
- Defensible gap: no budget-matched JS vs W1 vs MMD/OT comparison on a stock scVI backbone trained on real counts (docs/PAPER_PLAN.md section 2): SI-15 fits the scIB files as distributed (the counts layer as scIB fed scVI; no strict-count main run), so "trained on real counts" is dropped.
