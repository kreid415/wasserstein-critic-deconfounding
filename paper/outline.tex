\hd{Abstract}{200 words; 7 sentences}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item Adversarial batch correction trains an encoder against a discriminator predicting each cell's batch; a Wasserstein-1 (W1) critic, estimating the earth mover's distance between batches, is an alternative.

\item Pre-registered comparison, eight tasks of the single-cell integration benchmark (scIB): scVI plus JS discriminator, three W1 critics (reference, pooled, barycenter), maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) or Sinkhorn divergence; decoder with or without batch label.

\added{The case for the critic rests on theory. The W1 distance is continuous in the generator's parameters and differentiable almost everywhere under regularity conditions, which the JS divergence is not; for two distributions on disjoint parallel lines, JS stays at a constant $\log 2$ while W1 equals their offset \citep{arjovsky2017wasserstein}. When the two supports are disjoint, a perfect discriminator exists whose gradient is zero on both, so the generator stops learning \citep{arjovsky2017towards}. These results assume a fixed data distribution, a moving generator and a discriminator close to optimal. In batch correction the latent codes of every batch move at once and the adversary is updated only once or a few times per encoder step, so the assumptions need not hold. Whether batches are nearly separable before integration, and whether discriminator gradients vanish during training, are questions we examine descriptively rather than assume.}

Each setting runs at three strengths, its base method's matched strength on the main benchmark (the strength whose batch score is nearest $b^*$) and the two grid neighbours, so the follow-up starts only after the main benchmark is scored: 486 fits. One contrast per decoder is confirmatory, the spectrally normalised pooled critic minus the spectrally normalised discriminator with the critics' optimiser, both at one step, which differ only in the loss and its target. Biology is read at $b^*$ where a setting's three strengths bracket it, pooled with the same model, margin and 0.95 rule, with Holm's correction over the two results; the divergence is said to explain the main gap only if the primary comparison and this contrast have a declared direction of the same sign and every regularisation and step-count contrast is smaller (Supplementary Note 9). Because both arms of this contrast inherit strengths chosen for other settings, a pre-check of 36 fits (18 per arm, on the pilot tasks at seed 100) decides by a rule fixed in advance whether each arm's three strengths move one half-decade step up, move down or stay: \tbd{pre-check outcome for each arm}.}

\hd{Results}{1,500 words; 10 paragraphs}
\pp{Res-1}{We first report which fits completed and where batch removal could be matched.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule: a fit fails when its loss or latent values become non-finite; failed fits are counted, and the matched batch-removal level $b^*$ uses only strengths with no failed fit (Supplementary Note 5, Table S6).
\item \res{fits completed and failed per method and decoder, of 3,000 main fits (80 plain scVI, 40 scvi-tools adversarial classifier, 480 per penalty) plus 408 baseline fits.}
\item \res{low-signal pairs (gap between plain scVI's batch score and the highest batch score every penalty reaches under 0.02, so $b^*$ cannot be told apart from plain scVI); methods never reaching $b^*$.} Low-signal readings go to a separate table and are never pooled (Supplementary Note 5).
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-2}{The premise check reports how separable batches are before integration and how adversary gradients behave in training (Figure 2).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule: separability is the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC; 0.5 chance, 1 perfect) of a 15-nearest-neighbour batch classifier with five-fold cross-validation, run on the 80 plain-scVI embeddings (8 tasks, 2 decoders, 5 seeds) and averaged over batches; batches count as nearly separable when the AUC is at least 0.95 in all five seeds of a task and decoder (Supplementary Note 10).
\item Rule: the per-epoch logs of the 2,880 penalty fits give each adversary's gradient norm with respect to the embedding (before multiplying by strength) and, for the discriminator, its batch accuracy; a gradient vanishes early if it stays at or below 10\% of its own peak from within the first 10\% of epochs; no strengths or seeds are selected (Supplementary Note 10).
\item \res{separability per task (2a); gradient norm per epoch at preset strengths (2b); discriminator accuracy (2c).}
\item Exploratory, descriptive; no pooled test and no claim on whether the premise holds. Per-task detail in Supplementary Figure S3.
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-3}{Figure 3 shows each method's trade-off between batch removal and biology in every real task.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule: each method's curve joins plain scVI and its six strengths by their mean batch and biology scores over five seeds; the vertical line marks $b^*$ and the standard tools appear as points (Supplementary Note 5).
\item \res{curves: overlap, early ends, collapse.}
\item Simulations per task: Supplementary Figure S1. Per-metric curves: Supplementary Figure S5.
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-4}{The primary comparison reports biology kept at matched batch removal, reference critic minus discriminator, for each decoder (Figure 4a, Table 3).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule: biology at $b^*$ is read where each curve first reaches $b^*$, interpolating between neighbouring strengths; uncertainty comes from repeating the reading with each of the five seeds left out; a task is not evaluable if it is low-signal, if either curve never reaches $b^*$, or if fewer than four of the five readings exist (Supplementary Note 5).
\item Rule: the six real tasks are pooled in the model of the Methods statistics paragraph; a direction is declared at posterior probability of at least 0.95 beyond the 0.01 margin, practical equivalence at 0.95 within $\pm0.01$, otherwise inconclusive (Supplementary Note 6).
\item \res{pooled estimate, 95\% interval, three posterior probabilities, call; each decoder.}
\item \res{per-task estimates; evaluable tasks; Wilcoxon companion (descriptive: Holm over the six primary results, first threshold $0.05/6=0.0083$, cannot reject with six tasks); simulations per task (Supplementary Figure S1).}
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-5}{Trade-off area and collapse rate give a second and third view of the same comparison (Figure 4b,c, Table 3).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule, trade-off area: the area of the batch-score by biology-score plane that a method's six points reach beyond a corner at plain scVI's batch score and 0.10 below its biology score, divided by the batch gap that sets $b^*$; low-signal pairs are left out; reference critic minus discriminator, pooled as in Res-4 (Supplementary Note 7).
\item Rule, collapse: a fit is collapsed if it fails or its biology score is at least 0.10 below the same seed's plain scVI; the collapse rate is the collapsed share of 30 fits per method, task and decoder, with exact binomial (Clopper--Pearson) 95\% intervals; the reference critic's rate minus the discriminator's is pooled with a margin of 5 percentage points, and low-signal pairs are kept (Supplementary Note 7).
\item \res{trade-off area difference; collapse rate with intervals, pooled difference, call; per decoder.}
\item \res{sensitivity to prior scale (0.05 and 0.2 against 0.1) and to dropping low-signal tasks (Supplementary Note 6, Supplementary Figure S2).}
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-6}{We then report whether batch removal is more local with a critic, and the label-transfer check (Figure 4d).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule, local versus global: local removal is the mean of three neighbourhood metrics (integration local inverse Simpson's index, k-nearest-neighbour batch-effect test, graph connectivity) and global removal the mean of two whole-embedding metrics (principal-component regression, batch average silhouette width); the batch score is $(3\,\mathrm{local}+2\,\mathrm{global})/5$, so at $b^*$ more local removal means less global. The reference critic's local-minus-global reading minus the discriminator's is taken at the primary comparison's crossing and tested with an exact sign test over real tasks and with the pooled model (Supplementary Note 7).
\item \res{local-minus-global difference per decoder, sign test, pooled call.}
\item Rule, label transfer: each batch is held out in turn and a 15-nearest-neighbour classifier trained on the other batches predicts its cell types, on each method's embedding at its matched strength (batch score nearest $b^*$, never chosen by accuracy) for five seeds, with plain scVI and the scvi-tools adversarial classifier labelled unmatched; cell types found in one batch only are dropped; F1 is computed per cell type and averaged, with the gap between the matched batch score and $b^*$ printed beside it; no test or decision rule; low-signal pairs tabulated but left out of summaries (Supplementary Note 10, Supplementary Figure S4).
\item \res{label-transfer F1 per type (supplement), exploratory.}
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-7}{The range of good penalty strengths is reported for every method (Figure 5a).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule: quality is the selection score (0.4 batch plus 0.6 biology); for each method, task and decoder the best strength has the highest mean selection score over five seeds, the good range holds every strength of the six (Table 2, Supplementary Table S3) whose mean is at least the lower end of the best strength's 95\% confidence interval, and the width is the span from smallest to largest good strength in decades; descriptive, no decision rule (Supplementary Note 7).
\item \res{width in decades per method, task, decoder; best strength at grid edge; no good range.}
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-8}{The regularisation and update-step follow-up reports whether a critic--discriminator difference comes from the divergence (Figure 5b).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule, read-out at matched strength: each follow-up variant runs at its base method's matched strength from the main benchmark (batch score nearest $b^*$) and its two grid neighbours; biology at $b^*$ is interpolated along the three strengths where they bracket $b^*$, with all three leave-one-seed-out readings required, otherwise the task is not evaluable; for every task the difference at the matched strengths is also reported, labelled fixed-strength (Supplementary Note 9).
\item Rule, confirmatory contrast and explanation: per decoder, the spectrally normalised pooled critic minus the spectrally normalised discriminator with the critic's optimiser, both at one step; 0.01 margin and 0.95 rule as in Res-4; Holm's correction over its two results (companion test cannot reject with three tasks, smallest $p$ 0.25); the divergence is said to explain the main gap only if Res-4 has a declared direction for that decoder, this contrast has the same direction, and every regularisation and step-count contrast is smaller in size; no direction with fewer than three evaluable tasks; failure counts replace collapse rates because the follow-up has no plain-scVI fits (Supplementary Note 9).
\item \res{biology at matched strength minus plain discriminator, per variant and decoder; confirmatory contrast: estimate, interval, call.}
\item \res{one, five, ten critic steps, pooled critic.}
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-9}{Single-strength tables place each method against plain scVI and the standard tools (Table 4, Figure 6b).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule: each penalty is shown at a strength not tuned on that task, the one with the best mean selection score on real tasks of other families (related tasks never choose for each other), beside the task's own best strength as an optimistic bound; Harmony, Scanorama and sysVI are run at six settings each, scANVI (uses cell-type labels) and PCA (uncorrected reference) at one, five seeds for the neural methods; descriptive (Supplementary Notes 7 and 8, Table S4).
\item \res{selection score per method, decoder, task at strength from other families; own-best strength as optimistic bound; batch against biology score per real task.}
\end{itemize}
\pp{Res-10}{The comparison at equal numbers of tries reports whether any adversary improves on plain scVI (Figure 6a).}
\begin{itemize}
\item Rule: each method gets the expected best selection score among $k$ of its settings drawn at random, $k=1$--6, so methods with more settings gain no advantage; optimistic because the best setting is chosen and scored on the same data; scANVI has its own stratum (Supplementary Note 7).
\item \res{expected best selection score at $k=1$--6 per family and decoder; whether any adversary exceeds plain scVI, Harmony, Scanorama or sysVI.}
\item Descriptive; best-of-$k$ labelled optimistic.
\end{itemize}

\pp{Disc-3}{\res{guidance for scVI users, including the outcome that no adversary improves on plain scVI.}}
\begin{itemize}
\item \res{when a penalty is worth adding; strength range; whether a standard tool does better at equal tries.} Word from placement results only.
\end{itemize}
\pp{Disc-4}{The design limits what the data can show.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Underpowered (minimal detectable effect 0.131, six real tasks, 80\% power); Wilcoxon descriptive. Four families, two nested pairs (human--mouse immune contains all immune cells; large ATAC contains small).
\item Strength grids set on three of eight tasks. Reference critic five steps, discriminator one; update-step control only for pooled critic.
\item One batch variable per task; ATAC as gene-activity matrices; non-integer counts as distributed; scoring includes training cells; Sinkhorn not W1; reference choice uses labels.
\end{itemize}
\pp{Disc-5}{Several factors are outside the scope of this comparison.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Not varied or tested: architecture, KL warm-up (incomplete on shorter tasks, penalty from start), reference batch, batch number; composition shift read from strength curves, not a dose-response test. Adversary steps reuse one minibatch.
\end{itemize}
